# Domain: Papers, Reports, Articles

Applies to academic papers, student papers, technical reports, white papers, and long-form articles.

## 1. Structure that carries an argument

| Section | Job | Failure if missing |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract | Claim, method, result, implication, in that order | Reader cannot decide whether to read |
| Introduction | Context, gap, question, contribution | Reader does not know what is being solved |
| Related work | What exists, what it misses, how this differs | Novelty is asserted, not shown |
| Method | Enough detail to reproduce | Result is unfalsifiable |
| Results | Findings only, no interpretation | Interpretation contaminates evidence |
| Discussion | Meaning, alternative explanations, limitations | Reader supplies their own conclusions |
| Conclusion | Answer to the question, scope, next step | Ends with a summary instead of an answer |

Adapt to the required template when one is given. The jobs above do not change; the headings do.

## 2. Craft minimums

- One claim per section. A section advancing two claims gets split.
- Every paragraph presents evidence, interprets evidence, or transitions. Paragraphs doing none of these are filler.
- No orphan citations. A citation attaches to a specific claim, not to the end of a paragraph as decoration.
- Literature review synthesizes. "Author A found X. Author B found Y." is a list. "A and B agree on X but use incompatible samples, which explains Y" is a review.
- Method states data, sample, instruments, procedure, analysis, and threats to validity. Someone holding the data should be able to repeat the work.
- Limitations are specific. "This study has limitations" is noise. "The sample is 84 undergraduates at one university, so the effect may not generalize to professional developers" is a limitation.
- The conclusion answers the question asked in the introduction, in the same terms.

## 3. Citations

- Follow the required style exactly (APA 7, IEEE, Chicago, or the institutional template). When unspecified, APA 7 for social sciences and IEEE for engineering.
- Every in-text citation has a reference entry and every entry is cited. Check both directions.
- Cite the version you read. Preprint and published version can differ.
- Never invent page numbers, volume numbers, or DOIs. A missing field beats a fabricated one.
- Quote sparingly. Paraphrase with citation is usually stronger.

## 4. Common failure modes

- Literature dump with no synthesis.
- Method described as "data were analyzed" with no test named.
- Results section that argues; discussion section that repeats results.
- Conclusion introducing a claim the results do not support.
- Abstract promising more than the paper delivers.
- Citation padding to reach a reference count.

## 5. Verification before submission

- Every number in the abstract appears in the results.
- Every claim in the conclusion is supported earlier in the text.
- Every reference resolves and is cited in the body.
- Someone who did not do the work can repeat the method from the text alone.
- Word count, formatting, and section order match the requirement.
