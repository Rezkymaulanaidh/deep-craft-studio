# Domain: Slide Decks

Applies to PPTX, Marp, HTML slides, and any presented deck.

## 1. Deck contract

- One takeaway per slide. A slide that needs "and" is two slides.
- The headline states the claim, not the topic. "Checkout abandonment costs 22% of revenue" beats "Checkout Funnel".
- Body supports the headline with at most five short points, one visual, or one number.
- Detail lives in speaker notes. The slide is a visual aid, not a document.
- Slide count is roughly presentation minutes divided by 1.5. A 15-minute talk is about 10 slides.

## 2. Narrative arc

1. Situation: what is true now, with the number that makes it concrete.
2. Complication: what changed or what is broken.
3. Question: the decision the audience must make.
4. Resolution: the recommendation, with evidence.
5. Close: the ask, the next step, the date.

Cut any slide that does not advance this arc, even when it is interesting.

## 3. Data slides

- Choose the chart by question: comparison (bar), trend (line), distribution (histogram or strip), relationship (scatter), composition (stacked bar; avoid pie beyond three slices).
- Bar charts start at zero. Line charts may use a non-zero baseline when the variation matters; label the axis and state the choice.
- Label unit and period on the chart itself, not only in the notes.
- Every data slide carries a source line: source, period, n.
- Highlight one series. When everything is emphasized, nothing is.

## 4. Build hygiene for PPTX and HTML

- One type scale: title, body, caption. No fourth size invented per slide.
- Consistent margins and alignment grid. Text that jumps between slides reads as carelessness.
- Check contrast for projection: dark on light or light on dark, not mid-tones.
- No clip-art, no stock handshake photos, no decorative icons carrying no information.
- Alt text on images if the deck will be shared digitally.
- Render the deck to images and inspect every slide before delivery. Overflow and font substitution only show up visually.

## 5. Failure modes

- Wall of text, read aloud.
- Topic headlines that force the audience to infer the point.
- Bullet laundry with six levels of indentation.
- Unsourced numbers, especially round ones.
- Animation that delays the point.
- An appendix used to hide the argument instead of supporting it.

## 6. Verification before delivery

- Read only the headlines in order. They should form the argument.
- Every number has a source and a period.
- Every slide renders without overflow in the target format.
- Speaker notes are complete enough for someone else to present.
- Rehearse once against the clock.
